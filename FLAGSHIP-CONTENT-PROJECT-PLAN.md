# AutoAtlas Flagship Content Project: Selection and Execution Plan

**Prepared:** 2026-10-02  
**Status:** Proposal only; no content pages, data, or workflows were changed.

## Recommendation

Build an **AutoAtlas Regional EV Range Atlas**: a transparent, repeatable dataset that compares certified WLTP range with AutoAtlas road-test results for real highway and urban use, plus separate summer and winter results where conditions permit.

Start in **Jordan** and only include vehicles confirmed to be available in the selected model year and market. Expand to other Gulf or Arabic-speaking markets after the protocol and first season are established. The differentiator should be measured and clearly conditioned local-market evidence, not a claim that one test gives every driver’s range.

### Why #1 wins

Range is relevant to prospective owners, current owners planning trips, journalists, and researchers. The subject already attracts serious testing: ADAC's 2026 winter evaluation controlled temperature at 0°C and replayed a recorded road route on a test rig; InsideEVs reports a fixed-speed 70 mph highway protocol; Recurrent analyzes winter performance from a large connected-vehicle sample. AutoAtlas should not imitate those datasets without a clear difference. Its strongest potential difference is **vehicle/trim/market-specific testing in Jordanian operating conditions, published in Arabic and English first, with French and Portuguese summaries where useful**. Local climate and confirmed local vehicle supply should be treated as a research hypothesis to validate, not assumed to be an uncontested gap.

Demand is rated high directionally because official agencies publish separate range test explanations and labels, and specialist outlets produce repeated model-by-model range results. No numeric search volume is claimed here. Confirm query demand and local model availability in phase zero before buying equipment or committing to fleet size.

## Execution plan for project #1

### Phase 0 — Validate the opportunity (1–2 weeks)

1. Export AutoAtlas Search Console queries and pages related to range, WLTP, highway, city/urban, heat, cold, and charging. Record impressions, clicks, country, language, and query intent.
2. Check local registration/availability evidence and official manufacturer specifications for candidate models by model year, trim, battery, and market. Make a short list of 4 pilot vehicles across body size and powertrain variants.
3. Review local road/weather options and get written permission and insurance for each vehicle/test route. Set a budget and confirm access to reproducible energy measurement before scheduling field work.
4. Publish a short methodology note before results. Stop or narrow scope if vehicle access, safe testing, or measurement repeatability cannot be established.

**Gate:** Proceed only if there are enough locally relevant vehicle variants, a safe repeatable route, consistent charging/energy measurement access, and evidence of meaningful search or audience demand.

### Phase 1 — Protocol and pilot (4–6 weeks)

1. Define separate result classes:
   - **WLTP range:** quoted from the vehicle's official certification/specification for the exact variant; never presented as an AutoAtlas measurement.
   - **Real highway range:** a specified route and target speed/profile, with elevation, traffic, weather, and HVAC recorded.
   - **Urban range:** a defined route or cycle with speed distribution, stops, trip duration, and HVAC recorded.
   - **Summer range / winter range:** field results tagged with actual ambient conditions and test date; these are condition-specific observations, not separate universal certification standards.
2. Select and preregister the test variables: model year/trim, wheel/tire option, tire pressure, payload, drive mode, software version when available, SOC start/end, preconditioning, cabin setpoint, lights/accessory load, and route profile.
3. Run a small repeatability pilot on two vehicles. Repeat the same test on separate runs and estimate the spread before deciding what measurement uncertainty can be reported honestly.
4. Capture route GPS/elevation, time, ambient temperature, speed trace, wind/precipitation where practical, dashboard energy use, SOC, charging session start/end, energy supplied, and any deviations. Retain anonymized raw logs and a field notebook.
5. Have a qualified reviewer assess electrical measurement, data handling, vehicle safety, and the test procedure before expanding the sample.

**Method cautions:** Do not discharge vehicles to unsafe/stranding conditions merely to obtain a 100%-to-0% number. Prefer a declared SOC window and report observed distance and consumption. Any full-charge-equivalent figure must be labeled as a projection with its calculation and uncertainty. Do not compare a certified WLTP number directly with an AutoAtlas estimate without explaining their different protocols.

### Phase 2 — First release (8–12 weeks after the pilot)

1. Test 4–6 high-interest, locally confirmed variants first; expand toward 8–12 only if access, repeatability, and budget support it.
2. Repeat baseline highway and urban runs for every vehicle. Capture summer results when local ambient conditions are documented; plan winter retests in the actual winter season rather than extrapolating cold-weather results from another region.
3. Publish one flagship overview plus individual model pages. Every result should show exact variant, test date, market, conditions, observed SOC window, distance, energy consumption, and measurement method.
4. Provide downloadable anonymized CSV/JSON, route profile, protocol version, calculation notes, and a correction/change log. Preserve raw logs internally for audit.
5. Present per-vehicle results and uncertainty ranges; do not publish a single league table that implies statistical certainty from one run.

### Phase 3 — Build the durable asset (ongoing)

- Add a searchable comparison table and filters for body class, model year, drivetrain, tire/wheel variant, route, and ambient temperature.
- Keep WLTP, test results, and projected seasonal estimates visually distinct. Add tooltips for test cycle, usable SOC window, consumption, and uncertainty.
- Retest when a materially different model year, battery, tires, software calibration, or thermal system is introduced. Show historical values rather than silently overwriting them.
- Add Gulf-market expansions only as separate market records, with local trim and temperature data. Do not transfer Jordan results to another market as if they were measured there.
- Link the range dataset into the EV technologies, charging infrastructure, solar charging, and model comparison guides. This gives the project a coherent topic cluster and creates internal links back to its evidence source.

### Proposed Jordan content cluster

Use the following proposed URLs as connected companion studies. Each should have its own question, evidence, and methods; avoid publishing five near-duplicate explainers. They support the Regional EV Range Atlas, but require their own data collection and do not replace its vehicle-level range tests.

| Proposed page | Distinct editorial purpose | Connection to flagship |
|---|---|---|
| `solar-ev-jordan.html` | Quantify PV output and EV charging demand for a stated Jordan location, system size, orientation, tariff, and charging schedule; show seasonal and hourly matching. | Gives local climate/energy context for range and charging decisions; keep PV yield separate from driving-range evidence. |
| `jordan-ev-charging-study.html` | Establish a dated baseline for public/private charger locations, power, connector, uptime/availability evidence, tariffs, and geographic coverage. Prefer official regulator and operator data; map coverage only where coordinates and permissions support it. | Helps interpret route feasibility and choose repeatable range-test corridors. |
| `amman-public-transport-electrification.html` | Analyze one defined transit mode or corridor: duty cycle, route energy, charging window, depot power, fleet availability, and service constraints. | Extends vehicle-energy analysis from private cars to high-utilization transport without mixing the datasets. |
| `commercial-fleet-electrification-jordan.html` | Build a fleet-screening method using route/telematics demand, payload, dwell time, depot charging, grid capacity, and total-cost sensitivity. | Adds a commercial audience and a separate set of vehicle/range duty cycles for later fleet testing. |
| `home-energy-storage-smart-charging.html` | Model a specific household load, EV charging window, PV/storage configuration, round-trip efficiency, tariff, and backup objective. | Shows how charging availability and energy management affect ownership context; it is not a vehicle range test. |

**Sequencing:** first validate official data availability and define the geographic unit for the charging study; then develop the solar and home-energy case from the same weather/tariff assumptions; scope transit and commercial fleets separately with operator or route data. Cross-link only where pages share the same stated assumptions. Each page should carry its own last-updated date, sources, and an assumptions box.

### Core deliverables and success measures

**Deliverables:** public methodology, pilot report, model-specific result cards, interactive range table, source/condition metadata, anonymized downloadable data, and an annual update/change log.

**Measure success using:** non-branded organic impressions/clicks for the five range intents by language/market; citations and referring domains; time on result pages and filter usage; repeat visits/downloads; dataset updates per model year; and whether every published value can be traced to a source file or test record. Set numeric targets only after the Search Console baseline and pilot.

## Main risks and controls

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Results are mistaken for certification or guaranteed owner range. | Use clear method labels, conditions, confidence/uncertainty, and disclaimers adjacent to each figure. |
| A vehicle or route cannot be tested consistently. | Pilot and repeat first; narrow the variant list rather than infer missing measurements. |
| Weather, traffic, tires, and test settings confound comparison. | Record them, use a repeatable route/profile, publish deviations, and avoid cross-season causal claims from unmatched runs. |
| Brand/model selection creates selection bias. | Publish inclusion rules and market source; retain non-tested models as “not tested,” never silently exclude them from context. |
| Data is too sparse to support rankings. | Display the observations and sample size; wait for adequate replicates before ranking. |
| Market or query opportunity is assumed rather than verified. | Use the phase-zero Search Console and availability gate before commissioning a broad test fleet. |
