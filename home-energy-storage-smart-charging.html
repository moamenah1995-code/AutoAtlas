DISTRIBUTED ENERGY · DESIGN METHOD

A home battery is not automatically required for an EV or rooftop solar installation. Its value depends on when PV is produced, when the vehicle is parked, household load, export rules, tariffs, outage needs, and round-trip losses. Begin with interval data and a defined objective.

Energy flows and control priority

Measure or estimate hourly household demand and EV charging demand, then model PV generation at the same time resolution. A controller can prioritize house load, EV charging, stationary-battery charging, export, and reserve SOC. The grid remains the source during deficits unless the system is designed and permitted for islanded backup.

Worked storage sizing example

Illustrative assumptions: deliver 5 kWh to evening loads; 80% depth of discharge; 95% discharge-path efficiency; 80% retained capacity at the design end-of-life point. Required nominal capacity ≈ 5/(0.80 × 0.95 × 0.80) = 8.22 kWh. This is a simplified energy calculation, not a product recommendation; temperature, power limits, reserve SOC, warranty, degradation, and inverter standby consumption require further checks.

For a 90% AC-to-AC round-trip efficiency, storing 5 kWh of usable energy for later delivery requires approximately 5/0.90 = 5.56 kWh input. State the measurement boundary when comparing efficiency. Do not count storage losses twice in PV production estimates.

Size charging power separately from storage energy. A battery may have adequate kWh but insufficient kW to support a vehicle charger and household peaks simultaneously.

Smart charging and vehicle-grid options

Unidirectional managed charging (V1G) schedules charging based on departure needs, PV surplus, tariff periods, and a site import limit. Bidirectional V2H/V2B requires a compatible vehicle, EVSE, inverter/control path, protection, and permission to operate in the intended mode. Grid export is a separate V2G interconnection and market question, not an automatic function of bidirectional hardware.[1]

Jordan's MEMR discussed proposed changes in 2026 to the 2024 renewable connection system regarding storage under defined technical conditions. Homeowners and designers should confirm the effective rules, interconnection requirements, export treatment, and applicable tariff with the regulator and distribution company before procurement.[2]

Economic and resilience checks

  • Use actual bills and the current tariff schedule, not an assumed universal electricity price.
  • Calculate self-consumption and grid import/export from interval simulation; annual PV and EV totals alone cannot estimate savings.
  • Include battery round-trip loss, usable capacity, degradation, replacement, inverter, controls, warranty, and financing.
  • For backup, list critical loads, peak starting power, outage duration, reserve SOC, and islanding equipment. Grid-tied PV generally shuts down during an outage unless configured with approved islanding capability.
  • Compare storage against simpler controls: delayed EV charging, workplace charging, or increased PV self-consumption through scheduling.

A 10 kWh home battery: peak shifting is a power-and-energy problem

Battery capacity alone cannot prove a grid benefit. Usable energy, discharge power, household load shape, round-trip losses and tariff rules all determine whether a battery can reduce a coincident peak.

Did you know?

A 10 kWh battery is an energy rating; it does not say how many kW it can deliver or for how long at peak power.

Worked calculation

If 8 kWh delivered at peak avoids JD 0.18/kWh purchases, the avoided cost is JD 1.44. Recharging 8.89 kWh at JD 0.06 costs JD 0.53, leaving about JD 0.91 per full cycle before degradation, inverter standby, maintenance, financing and replacement. At 300 such cycles/year this is about JD 272/year; JD 5,000 ÷ 272 ≈ 18.4 years simple payback. Because the price spread and cost are scenario assumptions, this is a sensitivity—not an investment forecast. Smart charging (V1G) shifts timing without supplying energy back; V2H needs compatible vehicle, bidirectional EVSE, islanding/protection design and approval.

Interpretation and limits

A yearly energy total does not determine peak power, simultaneous demand, network voltage, or reliability. These calculations are reproducible planning examples based on the supplied manuscript. They are not measurements of a Jordanian operator, utility, or site. Confirm local tariffs, permits, equipment ratings, and connection conditions before design.
