Solar EV Charging in Jordan

A practical engineering method for sizing photovoltaic generation around vehicle charging demand, while keeping modeled output, grid exchange, and measured performance distinct.

ENGINEERING REFERENCE · ILLUSTRATIVE CALCULATION

Solar-powered vehicle charging is an energy-matching and electrical-integration problem. Array nameplate capacity alone does not tell you how many vehicle-kilometres solar electricity will supply: weather, module temperature, inverter conversion, shading, charging time, and grid rules all affect the result.

Start with a location-specific solar resource

For a Jordan site, obtain hourly or monthly irradiance and photovoltaic output for the coordinates, array tilt, azimuth, horizon, and technology. The World Bank's Jordan solar dataset exposes Global Solar Atlas layers including global horizontal irradiation, tilted-plane irradiation, and PV output. These are long-term modeled resource layers, not a guarantee for a roof or a particular year.[1] NREL PVWatts can provide a preliminary grid-connected PV estimate, but its output carries model assumptions and does not replace a detailed site survey or bankable design model.[2]

Inspect roof or canopy shading, dust/soiling, module temperature, inverter loading, wiring, availability, and curtailment. Do not combine an already loss-adjusted PV-output estimate with a second full performance-ratio deduction.

Worked example: daily and annual energy

Illustrative assumptions, not a Jordan site forecast: 5 kWDC array; 5.0 peak-sun-hours per day; aggregate performance ratio 0.80; 365-day resource held constant. Replace both resource and ratio with site-specific model inputs.

Daily AC generation ≈ 5 kW × 5 h/day × 0.80 = 20 kWh/day. Illustrative year-one energy ≈ 20 × 365 = 7,300 kWh/year. This simplified average hides seasonal and hourly variation, and is unsuitable by itself for battery or off-grid sizing.

For a vehicle driving 50 km/day at 18 kWh/100 km, traction-battery energy is 9 kWh/day. At an assumed 90% AC-meter-to-battery efficiency, the wall-side charging requirement is 10 kWh/day. Annual PV energy exceeds the annual EV requirement in this example; that does not mean the EV is solar-charged 100% of the time. A 12:00 solar surplus cannot directly serve a vehicle that returns home after sunset unless the energy is exported, stored, or charging is shifted.

Grid connection, storage, and charging controls

Model the system at hourly resolution where possible. For each interval, balance PV, building loads, EVSE demand, storage charge/discharge, grid import/export, and curtailment. Size the EVSE supply path for coincident electrical demand and protection requirements; do not size it from daily kWh alone.

Jordan's renewable-energy connection and wheeling/net-metering framework has changed over time. The 2024 regulation and subsequent 2026 ministry discussion of amendments are evidence that connection and storage provisions are jurisdiction- and date-sensitive. Confirm the current approved rules, utility connection study, meter configuration, export limit, and tariff with the regulator/distributor before using an economic model.[3][4]

Storage can move midday solar energy into an evening charging window, but it adds conversion loss, capital cost, degradation, controls, and safety requirements. Calculate usable energy from depth of discharge and conversion efficiency, then evaluate replacement and end-of-life assumptions. Smart charging can schedule vehicles to absorb solar surplus only when departure energy requirements remain satisfied.

Design checks and failure modes

  • Check inverter clipping, voltage rise, export constraints, reverse power flow, and protection coordination.
  • Model hot-module output and inverter thermal derating for the selected roof/canopy environment.
  • Include dust/soiling and cleaning water/operations assumptions; do not borrow a generic loss factor without stating it.
  • Check charger load diversity, vehicle dwell time, cable reach, accessibility, and safe separation from vehicle movement.
  • Compare grid-connected solar-assisted operation with off-grid operation. Off-grid design must meet poor-resource intervals and reserve autonomy, not merely average annual energy.

Decision rule: size the PV for energy and economics, the inverter and service for simultaneous power, and any battery for the time mismatch that actually exists in interval data.
