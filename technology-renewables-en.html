ENGINEERING OVERVIEW

Solar EV charging is a coupled generation, conversion, storage, and load-matching problem. Annual PV energy does not establish how much reaches a vehicle: irradiance varies by hour and season, conversion has losses, and charging must coincide with generation or be shifted through storage or the grid. The examples below are transparent screening calculations, not site guarantees.

Energy path and controls

In a common AC-coupled installation, PV power may serve the EVSE directly, charge a stationary battery through a bidirectional power conversion system (PCS), serve other building loads, or flow to/from the grid. The energy path is not necessarily serial through the battery: the energy management system (EMS) dispatches sources and loads subject to power limits, tariffs, and interconnection rules.

Worked engineering examples

1. Daily and annual PV production

Assumptions: 8 kWDC array; average POA irradiation 5.0 kWh/m²/day (5 peak-sun-hours); aggregate PR 0.80, including temperature, inverter, wiring, soiling, mismatch, and availability losses; 365-day average; no shading change. The PR is a single lumped factor, so its component losses must not be subtracted again.

Daily AC energy: 8 kW × 5 h/day × 0.80 = 32 kWh/day. Year-1 energy: 32 × 365 = 11,680 kWh/year. The implied DC-nameplate capacity factor is 11,680 ÷ (8 × 8,760) = 16.7%. With illustrative 0.5% annual degradation, year 10 is 11,680 × 0.9959 ≈ 11,167 kWh, assuming resource and availability otherwise unchanged.

2. EV charging demand

Assumptions: 40 km/day; vehicle battery consumption 18 kWh/100 km; 90% AC-meter-to-battery charging efficiency. Battery energy is 40 × 18/100 = 7.2 kWh/day. Wall energy is 7.2/0.90 = 8.0 kWh/day, or 2,920 kWh/year. Actual consumption changes with speed, temperature, HVAC, terrain, and vehicle.

3. Matching generation to charging

The 32 kWh/day PV average exceeds the 8 kWh/day EVSE load on an energy basis, but not necessarily during charging hours. Illustrative daily dispatch: 3 kWh of PV goes directly to the EVSE; 3.33 kWh charges an AC-coupled battery at 90% AC-to-AC round-trip efficiency, yielding 3 kWh later; the grid supplies the remaining 2 kWh. Thus 6/8 = 75% of EVSE energy is solar-supplied in this constructed day. It uses 6.33 kWh of PV for the EV and leaves 25.67 kWh of average PV generation for other site loads, export, or curtailment. The assumed 3 kWh direct overlap and storage dispatch require hourly data; annual totals alone cannot establish solar fraction.

Storage sizing and ageing

For 3 kWh delivered from a battery, assume 80% operating depth of discharge (DoD), 95% one-way discharge-path efficiency, and 80% end-of-life state of health (SOH). A conservative nameplate requirement is Enom ≥ 3 ÷ (0.80 × 0.95 × 0.80) = 4.93 kWh. DoD is the fraction of rated capacity cycled; SOH represents retained capacity relative to a reference condition. Round-trip efficiency is output energy/input energy across a stated AC or DC boundary; at 90% AC-to-AC, supplying 3 kWh requires 3/0.90 = 3.33 kWh input. Do not apply RTE a second time to the delivered energy. Calendar ageing, cycle throughput, temperature, SOC dwell, and operating power affect degradation; actual warranty limits and manufacturer data govern design. NREL defines residential storage RTE on an energy-in/energy-out basis (NREL ATB).

Lifecycle emissions: define the boundary

Compare equivalent transport service (for example, gCO₂e per vehicle-kilometre over a stated lifetime) and include the same boundary for each option. Separate PV module, inverter, mounting, and end-of-life emissions; stationary-battery manufacture and replacement; vehicle and traction-battery manufacture; electricity supply; and vehicle use. Grid electricity should use a geography- and time-appropriate factor where possible. For consequential questions, marginal generation may differ from an annual-average mix. Avoid counting exported PV both as avoided grid emissions and as low-carbon charging energy.

Example operational arithmetic only: at 18 kWh/100 km battery-side use and 90% charging efficiency, the meter supplies 20 kWh/100 km. At an assumed grid factor of 400 gCO₂e/kWh, that is 8,000 g/100 km = 80 gCO₂e/km, excluding vehicle, battery, PV, charger, and infrastructure manufacture. It is not a full lifecycle result. Use the IEA EV lifecycle calculator, IEA upstream factors, or Argonne GREET with matching boundaries. Peer-reviewed PV lifecycle analysis: Fthenakis & Kim, Environmental Science & Technology.

Economics: transparent illustrative case

PV levelized cost of energy (LCOE): assume $16,000 installed cost for the 8 kWDC PV system, $200/year O&M, 25-year life, year-1 output 11,680 kWh, 0.5% annual degradation, and no discounting, financing, inverter replacement, incentives, taxes, or battery cost. Lifetime generation is 11,680 × [1 − 0.99525]/0.005 ≈ 275,000 kWh. Undiscounted LCOE = ($16,000 + 25 × $200)/275,000 ≈ $0.076/kWh. A financial LCOE discounts annual costs and generation and includes replacements and financing; this screening figure is not a tariff.

Simple payback: assume the site consumes 80% of PV yield at an avoided retail price of $0.25/kWh and exports 20% at $0.06/kWh. Annual value = 11,680 × (0.8 × $0.25 + 0.2 × $0.06) = $2,476; less $200 O&M = $2,276/year. Payback = $16,000/$2,276 ≈ 7.0 years. This assumes that interval load can absorb 80% of generation; it excludes degradation, escalation, finance, tax, replacement, and battery capital. Actual bills may include demand charges and time-varying export compensation. IRENA's global utility-scale LCOE benchmarks are not rooftop site quotations (IRENA 2024 cost report).

Energy cost per driven kilometre: using the dispatch example, 3 kWh direct PV plus 3 kWh battery output requires 3.33 kWh PV input at 90% RTE; 2 kWh is grid energy. At PV LCOE $0.076/kWh and grid price $0.25/kWh, daily charging energy cost is 6.33 × $0.076 + 2 × $0.25 = $0.981. For 40 km, this is $0.0245/km (about $2.45/100 km), excluding storage capital/ageing, demand charges, taxes, and vehicle costs. It is an energy allocation calculation, not a household bill forecast.

Engineering questions

Does annual PV production equal solar EV charging?

No. Energy must coincide with charging or be shifted by storage. Use interval PV, building-load, EV arrival/departure, and export data to calculate self-consumption and solar fraction.

Why do nameplate PV and inverter ratings differ?

Modules are rated at DC standard test conditions; inverter output is AC and limited by its rating. A DC/AC ratio above one can improve annual inverter utilization while clipping some high-power intervals.

Can V2G make the installation profitable?

Not automatically. Revenue depends on market access, tariffs, availability, compatible equipment, interconnection rules, and degradation cost per battery throughput.
